# Alignment with NST2 and Vision 2050

The competition asks for solutions aligned with NST2 (2024 to 2029) and Vision 2050. This page records the link and what we could and could not verify. Source: the published NST2 document (sections cited below).

| NST2 element | What it says (paraphrased from the document) | How Ntawusigara supports it |
|---|---|---|
| Social Transformation Pillar, priority area 4.4, "Enhanced graduation from poverty and increased resilience" | Aims to reduce poverty significantly and to raise coverage and adequacy of social transfers to poor and vulnerable people (for example VUP) | Shows where poverty is highest and where it has stalled, how many poor people live in each district, and a coverage planner for sharing a programme across districts |
| Economic Transformation Pillar, priority area 3.9, "Resilient and broadened financial sector for private sector growth" | Lists "sustaining the expansion of financial inclusion" as an objective; the NST1 baseline is 96% inclusion by 2024 | Shows that inclusion is wide but shallow: where adults rely on informal finance only, or have no bank account, even though national inclusion is high |
| Cross-cutting: evidence-based interventions | The competition frames data solutions as supporting evidence-based interventions | District ranking with uncertainty, robustness tests, and a "What changed?" tool for the next survey round |

Vision 2050 sets the long-term aim of high living standards and an inclusive economy. Ntawusigara contributes by pointing both poverty programmes and financial-inclusion programmes at the same places, so that nobody is excluded from both.

## What we could not verify

The parts of NST2 we read state no numeric district-level target for poverty or financial inclusion, and the monitoring matrix (Annex 1) was not available to us. We therefore make **no claim about meeting a numeric NST2 target**. If your team obtains the M&E matrix, add the exact indicators and targets here and consider showing them next to the district figures.
